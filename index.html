Learning Theory Connections in Public Speaking

The connections in this concept map are based on ideas and approaches accumulated over 20 years of teaching public speaking.

Inexperienced public speakers tend to focus on form (Where do I look? What do I do with my hands? etc.) much more than on content (Why does what I’m saying matter to the people I’m saying it to?). They do this because they assume that the content is just the details of the already-determined information they’re going to convey, but people don’t crave information, they crave meaning. Strong form flows from a personal connection to what is meaningful in the content. When a speaker is focused on delivering content they believe is meaningful, their form changes because the feeling that they are offering something of value to their audience naturally leads them to engage in effective speaker behaviours like smiling, making eye contact, and using open-handed gestures.

Given enough practice, anyone can significantly improve their public speaking skills.
